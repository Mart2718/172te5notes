Law of Sines (theorem)

From the handout

Theorem. The Law of Sines says the ratio of each side of a triangle to the sine of its opposite angle is the same for all three sides:

a/(sin A) = b/(sin B) = c/(sin C)   or   sin A/a = sin B/b = sin C/c

When using the Law of Sines, you will only need to set one fraction equal to one other fraction. Note that these are proportions and so the reciprocals also hold true.

Note: when to use the Law of Sines

From the handout

The Law of Sines is helpful when we have

  • two angles and one of their corresponding sides, or
  • two sides and one of their corresponding angles.

Observation: the ambiguous case

From the handout

In the previous three activities, we saw that when we use the Law of Sines to find an angle, an ambiguity can arise due to the sine function being positive in Quadrant I and Quadrant II. In other words, if two sides and the non-included angle are given (SSA), three situations may occur. The Ambiguous Case of the Law of Sines states that when using the Law of Sines to find a missing angle, there may be two possible solutions.

  • NO triangle exists (no solution)
  • TWO different triangles exist (2 solutions)
  • Exactly ONE triangle exists (1 solution)

Your handout says “missing side length” here. It's the missing angle that can have two values.

Law of Cosines (theorem)

From the handout

Theorem. The Law of Cosines relates the lengths of the sides of a triangle to the cosine of one of its angles. In other words, the square of any one side of a triangle is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides, minus twice the product of those two sides and the cosine of the angle between them.

Let a, b, and c be the lengths of the three sides of a triangle and A, B, and C be the three angles. Then:

a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A
b² = a² + c² − 2ac cos B
c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C
